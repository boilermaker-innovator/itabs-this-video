# Working at heights: toolbox talk

**Sample toolbox talk · made up for this demo, not official training**

*Sample notes, packaged by iTabs · itabs-this-video.jgwynne7.workers.dev*

> **Who it's for:** anyone on site who uses ladders, scaffolds, roofs or elevated work platforms, and the people who supervise them.

> General information only. Always follow your site's procedures, your Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS) and your supervisor.

---

## Your homework

**Before your next job at height, walk the area with your supervisor and agree how a fall will be prevented.**

You're done when you can point to every spot where someone could fall, and name the control for each one.

---

## The big idea

**Stop the fall from happening. Don't just plan to survive it.**

Falls from height are one of the biggest causes of deaths and serious injuries at work. Many happen from low heights on routine jobs, like a ladder, a roof edge or the back of a truck.

## Key points

1. **Work from the ground if you can.** The safest fall is the one that can't happen. Pre-assemble on the ground, use extension poles, or move the job.
2. **Use fixed protection before harnesses.** Guardrails, scaffolds and elevated work platforms stop a fall without anyone having to do anything right.
3. **A harness is the last line of defence.** It needs a rated anchor, training, an inspection before use, and a rescue plan before anyone clips on.
4. **Ladders are for short, light jobs.** Keep three points of contact, set the ladder at about 1 out for every 4 up, secure it, and don't stand on the top step or top rungs.
5. **Protect the people below.** Set up exclusion zones, use toe boards and tool lanyards, and never throw tools or materials down.

## The order of controls

| Control | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Eliminate | Do the job from the ground |
| Passive protection | Guardrails, scaffold, elevated work platform |
| Work positioning | Restraint that stops you reaching the edge |
| Fall arrest | Harness and anchor that catch a fall |
| Admin controls | Procedures, training, supervision |

> **What a "SWMS" is:** a Safe Work Method Statement. In Australia, construction work where someone could fall more than 2 metres is high risk construction work, and it needs a SWMS before the work starts.

> **Why rescue plans matter:** a person hanging in a harness after a fall needs to be rescued quickly. Know who will get them down, and how, before work starts.

Help me apply this to my own work, one step at a time:
