# Electrical safety basics for apprentices

**Sample lesson · made up for this demo, not official training**

> **Who it's for:** apprentices and new starters who work around electrical equipment or near electrical work.

> General awareness only. This is not a guide to doing electrical work. In Australia, electrical work must be done by a licensed electrician, or by an apprentice under their supervision. Always follow your site's procedures and your supervisor.

## The big idea

**Treat everything as live until it's been proven dead.**

Electricity gives no warning. A switch that's off, a circuit someone says is dead, or a tool that looks fine can still hurt you. Checking first is what keeps people safe.

## Key points

1. **Test before you touch.** Never assume something is dead. Electrical workers prove it with a tester that has been checked on a known live source before and after the test.
2. **Isolate, lock and tag.** Equipment is switched off at its isolation point, locked with a personal lock, and tagged with the person's name. Never remove someone else's lock or tag.
3. **Know your RCDs.** An RCD (safety switch) cuts power fast if current leaks to earth, for example through a person. Use one with portable tools, and check it's been tested. It reduces the risk but doesn't make unsafe work safe.
4. **Check leads and tools before use.** Look for damaged cords, plugs and casings, and check the test tag is in date. Don't use anything damaged. Tag it out and report it.
5. **When in doubt, stop and ask.** If something is unexpected, damaged, wet, smells like burning, or keeps tripping a safety switch, stop and ask your supervisor. Never bypass a safety device.

## Lockout/tagout at a glance

| Part | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| Isolate | The energy source is switched off at the right point |
| Lock | A personal lock stops anyone switching it back on |
| Tag | The tag says who is working on it, and why |
| Test | Proves it's dead before anyone touches it |

> **What "test-prove-test" means:** checking the tester works on a known live source, testing the circuit, then checking the tester again, so a faulty tester can't fool you. Your supervisor or trainer will show you how it's done on your site.

> **What an RCD is:** a residual current device, often called a safety switch. It watches the current going out and coming back. If some is leaking, for example through a person, it cuts the power in a fraction of a second.
