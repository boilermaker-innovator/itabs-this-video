# Welding basics for apprentices

> **Who it's for:** apprentices and new starters doing or working near welding, and the people who supervise them.

> General awareness only. Learn to weld from a qualified trainer, and follow your workplace's procedures and your supervisor.

## Your homework

**Before your next welding session, check your PPE, your equipment and the area with your supervisor.**

You're done when your supervisor has looked over your set-up and you've fixed anything they flagged.

---

## The big idea

**Good welds start with a safe, clean, well-prepared job.**

Most welding problems, from injuries to weak welds, come from skipping the set-up. Protect yourself and others, prepare the metal, then weld.

## Key points

1. **Protect your eyes and skin.** Wear a welding helmet with the right shade lens, leather welding gloves, flame-resistant clothing and closed boots. The arc's UV light burns skin and eyes.
2. **Keep fumes out of your lungs.** Welding fume is classified as able to cause cancer. Use fume extraction or good ventilation, keep your head out of the plume, and wear respiratory protection when required.
3. **Stop fires before they start.** Sparks travel a long way. Clear flammable materials, have an extinguisher ready, and follow your site's hot work permit and fire watch rules.
4. **Check the electrics.** Inspect leads, the electrode holder or torch, and the earth clamp before you start. Keep gloves dry and don't weld in wet conditions.
5. **Prepare the metal.** Clean off rust, paint and oil, line the parts up well, tack them in place, and set the machine for the metal and its thickness.
6. **Protect the people around you.** Use welding screens so others don't get arc flash, and warn people before you strike an arc.

## Common welding processes

| Process | What it's known for |
|---------|---------------------|
| MIG (GMAW) | Wire fed through a gun with shielding gas. Quick to learn, common in fabrication |
| TIG (GTAW) | Tungsten electrode with a separate filler rod. Precise, good for thin metal, stainless and aluminium |
| Stick (MMAW) | Flux-coated electrode. Works outdoors and for repairs |

> **What "arc eye" is:** a painful eye injury from the arc's UV light, a bit like sunburn of the eye. It can take hours to show up, and it can happen to people standing nearby, not just the welder.
